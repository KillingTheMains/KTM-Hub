Read this before you pull cable. Distro Plot is a planning aid, not a design document, and it is not a substitute for a licensed electrician or the AHJ. Fixture draws are published nominal maximums — a fixture in a hot room, at low line voltage, or running a pixel-mapped chase can pull more.
What the numbers assume. Ampacity follows NEC 310.16 (75 °C column). Voltage drop uses the K-factor approximation — K = 12.9 Cu / 21.2 Al, VD = K·I·L·multiplier ÷ circular mils, ×2 for a two-wire branch and ×√3 for a three-phase feeder — which ignores conductor temperature, power factor angle and raceway effects. The 3 % branch / 5 % combined drop figures are NEC Informational Notes (210.19(A) IN No. 4, 215.2(A)(1) IN No. 2), non-enforceable under 90.5(C) — but some AHJs adopt them locally, and 110.3(B) makes a fixture's own minimum-voltage spec enforceable regardless.
Neutral current is the fundamental-only unbalance of line-to-neutral loads, Iₙ = √(Iₐ²+I_b²+I_c²−IₐI_b−I_bI_c−I_cIₐ). Line-to-line loads have no neutral connection and are excluded. This ignores triplen harmonics, which on non-linear LED loads add arithmetically in the shared neutral rather than cancelling. Portable cable falls under NEC Article 400, not 310.16. Motor inrush of 3–6× is not modelled.
